# Physics-held grabbables and missing furniture colliders (v2)

This pass checks the fixes made after v1. The chair seat colliders now top out at 0.515 m, above the rig's 0.5 m step offset. `HeldObjectPhysics` clamps the held body to 6 m/s, so a blocked hand no longer asks for a 100 m/s velocity. The installer also runs on `SceneManager.sceneLoaded`. Six PlayMode tests were added, including a held object stopped by a wall and a pan lowered onto the stove. The compile check reports 0 errors in runtime, PlayMode and EditMode.

Watch for: (1) none of the 11 PlayMode tests has been executed because the Editor is open, so the wall and stove tests are unproven (confirmed). (2) The seat fix leaves a 1.5 cm margin over the step offset and has no test, so whether the player can still climb a chair is unverified (likely). (3) `KnifeSlicer` measures swipe speed from `transform.position` deltas per physics step, and the held knife is now physics-driven, interpolated and speed-clamped, so slicing may feel different (possible, check by hand).

**Verdict**: APPROVED

## High-level view

The speed clamp runs in the component's `FixedUpdate`, after XRI's interaction manager (execution order -105) has written the tracking velocity and before the physics step. Throw velocity comes from the hand and is not affected by it. Only linear speed is limited.

The seat colliders are now 9 cm slabs spanning 0.425 to 0.515 m, which clears the 0.5 m step offset by 1.5 cm. Anything placed on a seat rests 4 cm above the drawn seat. The author accepted that trade-off over editing the rig.

The held-object lag is deliberately not fixed, with a stated reason: `predictedVisualsTransform` moves a child that `KnifeSlicer` clones. It can only be judged in a headset.

The new tests have not run, so the wall-blocking and stove-snap behavior is still unproven.

## Speed clamp and the blocked-hand case

XRI's velocity tracking sets velocity to distance divided by the physics step. When a wall stops the object but the hand keeps going, that request grows without bound (2 m of hand travel asks for 100 m/s). The clamp caps the linear speed at 6 m/s while held, so a blocked pan does not launch plates or bread it touches. A swing faster than 6 m/s makes the object trail the hand until the hand slows down.

Rotation tracking works the same way and has no equivalent limit, so a blocked rotation produces a large angular velocity request. Contact between a spinning blocked pan and loose objects can still impart large impulses. Confidence: possible. It needs a hand test with a pan pressed against a wall.

## Knife swipe speed with a physics-driven held body

`KnifeSlicer` measures speed from how far `transform.position` moved between two `FixedUpdate` calls and gates cutting at 1.2 m/s. Before this change the held knife was teleported by XRI during the render update. Now it is a dynamic body that follows the hand through velocities, is interpolated, and is capped at 6 m/s. What `transform.position` returns inside `FixedUpdate` for an interpolated body, and how closely the knife follows the hand during a swipe, both change the measured value. v1 stated that no code path in the knife depends on the held body being kinematic. This one depends on how the body moves, so that check did not cover it. Confidence: possible. The check is to slice items in the Editor simulator or a headset and watch the `debugLog` speed readout.

## Chair seat collider geometry and the step offset

The seat is drawn at y 0.45 with scale y 0.05, so the visible slab spans 0.425 to 0.475 m. The collider (size y 1.8, center y 0.4 in local units) spans 0.425 to 0.515 m in world units. The rig's CharacterController has stepOffset 0.5, so the 1.5 cm margin is the only thing keeping the seat unclimbable, and nothing automated checks it. Confidence: likely.

Not tested: the `sceneLoaded` installer path, the chair step offset, the knife swipe speed gate, and every scene collider change (`Wall_Back`, `Chair_B`, the 14 BoxColliders).
